# Longevity evidence rubric

## Evidence grade

- **High:** replicated randomized evidence or strong outcome-based synthesis in a relevant human population, with consistent safety data.
- **Moderate:** reasonably direct human evidence with limitations unlikely to reverse the overall conclusion.
- **Low:** small, short, observational, surrogate-only, inconsistent, or indirect human evidence.
- **Very low:** animal, cellular, mechanistic, case report, uncontrolled experience, influencer testimony, or manufacturer claim.

## Decision modifiers

Downgrade confidence for:

- surrogate endpoints without validated clinical meaning;
- short follow-up for a long-term claim;
- selective reporting, multiplicity, high dropout, or missing adverse-event data;
- proprietary formulation without replication;
- dose or population mismatch;
- preprint or abstract without peer-reviewed full methods;
- large commercial conflicts or absence of independent replication.

## Source ladder

- NIH Office of Dietary Supplements fact sheets: https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/list-all/
- PubMed and Clinical Queries: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/
- ClinicalTrials.gov for registration and results: https://clinicaltrials.gov/
- PMDA for Japanese labels and safety information: https://www.pmda.go.jp/
- FDA safety communications for products regulated in the United States: https://www.fda.gov/safety

## Stoplight decision

- **Green:** likely benefit or useful symptom support, acceptable risk, and ordinary monitoring.
- **Yellow:** uncertain or modest benefit; consider only after interaction review and a defined stop rule.
- **Red:** no credible human benefit, material risk, deceptive marketing, illegal/unapproved use, or an invasive experimental intervention outside a legitimate trial.
