# Medical safety and language-support checklist

Use this checklist for educational design. It is not legal advice and does not replace the rules of the facility where the learner works. Apply Japanese law, professional scope, orders, and facility policy first.

## Risk tiers

| Tier | Typical content | Design action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| routine | greeting, room orientation, comfort, ordinary vital-sign explanation | Teach concise patient-facing English and ordinary role limits. |
| caution | symptom questions, mobility, IV checks, routine medication or procedure explanation, discharge details | Add closed-loop confirmation, avoid promises, and identify the local-policy dependency. |
| high | consent, serious diagnosis or prognosis, surgery, anesthesia, transfusion, resuscitation or code status, acute deterioration, high-risk medicine, major discharge risk | Teach immediate escalation and qualified language support. Do not simulate independent completion of the high-stakes discussion. |

## Lesson safety gate

- [ ] The learning goal is communication, not diagnosis, prescribing, consent, or autonomous decision-making beyond the nurse's role.
- [ ] The clinical sequence is plausible and does not imply that a normal-sounding response rules out danger.
- [ ] Urgent symptoms trigger immediate help, monitoring, and facility procedure rather than a long English lesson.
- [ ] Medication content follows an order and avoids dose invention, ambiguous timing, unsafe abbreviations, and promises about effect or side effects.
- [ ] Numbers, units, laterality, allergies, fasting, timing, and negatives are explicit and protected from bad line breaks.
- [ ] Patient-friendly words appear before technical terms.
- [ ] The lesson says when to stop using simple English and connect to the doctor, qualified interpreter, or facility language service.
- [ ] The Japanese translation carries the same risk level and does not soften a warning or strengthen a diagnosis.
- [ ] The page includes a local-policy disclaimer when workflow varies by facility.
- [ ] A clinically qualified human reviewer approves the final sequence and wording.

## Interpreter decision gate

Use a qualified interpreter or the facility-designated language service when the information is important, complex, sensitive, consent-related, or likely to cause harm if misunderstood. Avoid relying on untrained staff, family, friends, or minors as the default interpreter. In an emergency, prioritize immediate care and activate the facility's fastest approved communication support.

Teach calm bridge phrases such as:

- I want to make sure we understand each other clearly.
- I'm going to arrange a qualified medical interpreter.
- This information is important. We will use an interpreter.
- I'll contact the doctor now.
- Help is on the way. Stay with me.

Do not imply that bilingual ability alone makes someone a qualified medical interpreter.

## Teach-Back gate

- [ ] Frame Teach-Back as a check of the clinician's explanation, not a test of the patient.
- [ ] Ask for the plan in the patient's own words or use Show-Me for a practical skill.
- [ ] Do not rely only on Do you understand? or Does that make sense?
- [ ] If misunderstanding appears, explain again differently and check again.
- [ ] Break complex information into small segments rather than waiting until the end.

Preferred frame:

    I want to make sure I explained it clearly. Could you tell me how you'll take this medicine at home?

## Human release sign-off

Require all of the following:

- clinical content reviewer: approved, name, credential or role, date
- English reviewer: approved, name, qualification or role, date
- local-policy assumptions: listed and confirmed or clearly disclaimed
- unresolved medical or language-support issue: none

## Official reference points

- [MHLW materials on medical interpreting](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/stf/seisakunitsuite/bunya/0000056944.html): use as a Japanese reference point for medical-interpreter practice and training materials.
- [MHLW manual for receiving foreign patients](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/stf/seisakunitsuite/bunya/0000173230_00003.html): check the current Japanese healthcare-institution workflow and risk-management guidance.
- [MHLW multilingual explanation materials](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/stf/seisakunitsuite/bunya/kenkou_iryou/iryou/kokusai/setsumei-ml.html): use current approved materials when they fit the situation rather than recreating high-stakes explanations from memory.
- [AHRQ Teach-Back Method](https://www.ahrq.gov/health-literacy/improve/precautions/tool5.html): check how clearly information was explained; use non-shaming, open-ended Teach-Back and repeat clarification when needed.
- [HHS National CLAS Standards](https://thinkculturalhealth.hhs.gov/clas/standards): provide understandable care, offer language assistance, and ensure the competence of language-support personnel.
- [HHS guidance on family or friends as interpreters](https://www.hhs.gov/civil-rights/for-individuals/faqs/may-an-lep-person-use-a-family-member-as-an-interpreter/709/index.html): important medical decisions and consent call for competent interpretation; family or friends may be unable to ensure accurate understanding.

Treat the US sources as transferable safety references for communication design, not as statements of Japanese law. Recheck every official page during production and record the access date; the links were last checked while authoring this skill on 2026-08-23.
