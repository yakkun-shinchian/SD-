# SBAR handoff for novice nurses

Use this reference when a lesson teaches acute reporting, handoff, escalation, or SBAR. SBAR is a communication scaffold, not a diagnostic algorithm and not a substitute for emergency activation, local protocol, or clinical judgment.

## Teach four questions before four letters

| Letter | Novice-friendly question | What belongs here | Acute-change example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S — Situation | 今、何が起きている？ | Patient, immediate problem, urgency | 「斎藤さんに新しい右腕麻痺と構音障害があります」 |
| B — Background | ここまで、どうだった？ | Only relevant background, last known well, recent procedure or treatment | 「23:35は正常、23:47に発症。コイル塞栓術後2日目です」 |
| A — Assessment | 私が見た事実は何？ | Observations, numbers, direction of change; separate fact from inference | 「右腕麻痺と構音障害が持続。血糖112、血圧186/102です」 |
| R — Recommendation/Request | 何をしてほしい？ | Concrete request, urgency, read-back or next review time | 「至急診察をお願いします。院内手順の起動を確認してください」 |

In Japanese nursing education, explain `A` first as **観察した事実と自分の見立て**. A novice who is not ready to offer a diagnosis can still report precise findings and say, 「急変を疑っています」. Explain `R` as **相手への命令ではなく、患者安全のために必要な次の行動を明確に頼む部分**.

## One-breath first report

When time is critical, lead with S and R rather than waiting to complete a perfect four-part speech:

> 「斎藤さんに新しい右腕麻痺と構音障害があります。至急来て評価してください。」

Add B and A while help is being mobilized. If the receiver does not respond, repeat the urgency, use closed-loop confirmation, and escalate through the facility's chain of command.

## Comic teaching pattern

Show SBAR as four visible boxes or four short turns, not as one dense balloon.

- Balloon 1: `S：今の問題`
- Balloon 2: `B：直前までの経過`
- Balloon 3: `A：観察と数値`
- Balloon 4: `R：具体的な依頼`

Keep each item to one idea and two or three short lines. On the explanation page, pair every letter with its novice-friendly question. Include one completed example using the same patient facts as the comic.

## Safety checks

- Do not delay an emergency call in order to assemble SBAR.
- Do not present a diagnosis as confirmed unless it has been confirmed in the story.
- Protect laterality, times, numbers, units, negatives, and changes from bad line breaks.
- Keep the request concrete: who should come, what should be assessed or activated, and how urgently.
- Require clinical human review and state that local rapid-response, stroke, and escalation procedures take priority.

## Official reference point

- [AHRQ TeamSTEPPS 3.0 - Tool: SBAR](https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/curriculum/communication/tools/sbar.html): defines SBAR as Situation, Background, Assessment, and Recommendation or Request and describes its use for concise team communication, including rapidly deteriorating patients.
